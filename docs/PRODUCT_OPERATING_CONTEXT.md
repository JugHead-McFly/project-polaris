# Polaris Product Operating Context

Last updated: 2026-07-23

## Working relationship

Codex supports Polaris as a product-development partner and practical
chief-of-staff for the project. The role is broader than implementing code: it
keeps engineering, product, customer research, branding, launch preparation,
and business planning moving in the same direction.

The recurring operating questions are:

- What is the highest-value next step?
- What should Polaris work on this month?
- What can wait until evidence shows it is needed?
- What is the largest current risk?
- Which decisions should be prepared before they become blockers?

The default principle is to avoid solving problems before Polaris has evidence
that they are real. Build and business decisions should be staged, reversible
where practical, and proportional to the current milestone.

The product source of truth is [PRODUCT_BIBLE.md](PRODUCT_BIBLE.md). It records
the mission, principles, customer evidence, feature decisions, milestones, and
deliberate deferrals.

The guidance standard is evidence over reassurance. Codex must identify facts,
inferences, hypotheses, unknowns, and material risks; correct earlier advice
when new evidence changes it; and never present optimism as a forecast or
guarantee.

that reduces blank-page consulting time without pretending that preparation is
professional legal, tax, or accounting advice.

## Professional review boundaries

Use a qualified attorney for decisions with legal consequences, including:

- Final Terms of Service and Privacy Policy review.
- Trademark clearance and registration.
- Ownership or partnership agreements.
- Patentability and patent-strategy advice.

Use a qualified CPA or tax professional for:

- Tax filings and tax positions.
- Entity tax elections.
- Deduction eligibility.
- Year-end accounting and filing decisions.

Codex may organize questions and draft working materials for review, but must
identify these boundaries rather than presenting legal or tax conclusions as
professional advice.

## Day 1 customer research

Initial validation should observe how smart-telescope users plan an imaging
night before Polaris promotes a proposed solution.

Research exercise:

1. Spend 15–20 minutes observing recent Dwarf 3 and other smart-telescope
   community discussions.
2. Record recurring planning frustrations such as weather, target selection,
   Moon conditions, framing, and switching among multiple apps or websites.
3. Ask:

   > For those of you using a Dwarf 3 or another smart telescope, how do you
   > decide what you're going to image on a particular night? I'm especially
   > curious how many different apps or websites you normally check before
   > starting.

The purpose is workflow discovery, not product promotion. Findings should feed
the roadmap only when a pain point recurs often enough to justify attention.
The complete staged plan is in
[CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY_SPRINT.md](CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY_SPRINT.md).

## Competitive positioning

Polaris has direct and near-direct competitors. Do not describe the category as
empty or claim that no comparable app exists. Current store listings show
products with ranked targets, astronomy weather, night-quality scores,
equipment-aware planning, location discovery, integration calculators, AI
guidance, and session logs.

Polaris should compete on the quality of its decision loop: turn the user's
conditions, equipment, available time, actual capture history, integration
progress, and explainable image analysis into a specific recommendation for
what to do next. “What should I do tonight—and why?” is the core product
question.

See [COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md](COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md) for the evidence,
positioning, limitations, and recommended validation work.
