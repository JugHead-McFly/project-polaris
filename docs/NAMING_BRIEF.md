# Polaris Naming Brief

Last updated: 2026-07-23

## Current status

**Project Polaris** remains an internal working name. It is not a selected public
brand name, and the current logo should be treated as temporary. The name has
strong astronomical meaning but is crowded across adjacent product categories,
so it should not be assumed available for an app or company.

## Creative direction

The public name should feel clear, capable, welcoming, and credible to someone
new to astrophotography while still feeling at home with experienced users. The
initial exploration covers historic astronomy, navigation and exploration,
mythology, Latin and Greek roots, constellations and deep sky, discovery,
light and optics, AI and technology, invented names, and premium modern names.

## Workbook and review process

The first workbook contains 175 creative candidates and a family voting sheet.
The first-pass creative scores are subjective aids for discussion only. They
are **not** evidence of trademark availability, domain availability, app-store
availability, social-handle availability, or search uniqueness.

1. Family members mark candidates as Love, Like, Maybe, or Pass.
2. Doug brings back a combined top 10–15.
3. Polaris conducts preliminary web, app-store, domain, social-handle, and
   USPTO searches on the finalists.
4. A trademark attorney reviews the final one or two choices before public
   launch, company formation under the name, paid brand work, or filing a
   trademark application.

The candidate PDF is generated by
`scripts/generate_naming_workbook.py` and is kept at
`output/pdf/Polaris_Naming_Workbook_v1.pdf`.

## Decision rule

Do not publicly announce a new name or build permanent brand assets until the
shortlist has cleared the preliminary screen and legal review has been obtained
where appropriate. This protects the project from investing in a name that is
already confusingly similar to an existing product.
